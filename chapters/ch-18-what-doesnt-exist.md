> *"The absence of an instrument is not merely a constraint — it is a structural fact that alters every price in the market and every strategy available to every participant."*

Understanding what is missing from the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) is just as critical as understanding what is present. The absence of specific financial instruments dictates market volatility, shapes risk management practices, and preserves unique informational advantages for disciplined fundamental investors.

## Lesson 18.1 — Derivatives: Why NEPSE Operates as a Cash-Only Spot Market

NEPSE operates exclusively as a **cash spot market** with physical T+2 settlement. There are no exchange-traded equity futures, index futures, or put/call options.

```
                           THE ONE-DIRECTIONAL MARKET DILEMMA
   ┌───────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Markets With Derivatives          │ NEPSE Long-Only Cash Market       │
   ├───────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
   │ • Portfolios can buy put options  │ • Portfolios cannot hedge risk    │
   │   to hedge downside risk.         │   without outright selling.       │
   │ • Short sellers cover on dips,    │ • Bear markets drop sharply as    │
   │   softening sharp sell-offs.      │   panicked investors exit at once.│
   │ • Income generated via calls.     │ • Returns depend solely on gains. │
   └───────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Preconditions for Derivatives on NEPSE
1. Real-time marking-to-market clearinghouse and settlement guarantee fund.
2. Robust margin call and auto-liquidation infrastructure.
3. Market makers providing continuous, liquid two-sided bid/ask quotes.

## Lesson 18.2 — ETFs: The Absence of Low-Cost Passive Indexing

Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) track an underlying index with continuous intraday liquidity and sub-0.20% expense ratios, kept pegged to Net Asset Value via **Authorized Participants (APs)**.

```
                             MUTUAL FUNDS VS. TRUE ETFS
   ┌───────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Nepali Mutual Funds               │ Global Exchange-Traded Funds      │
   ├───────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
   │ • Closed-end trade at 15–25%      │ • AP arbitrage keeps price pegged │
   │   discounts to NAV on NEPSE.      │   directly to real-time NAV.      │
   │ • 1.0% to 2.0% Annual Expense.    │ • Ultra-low expense (0.05%–0.20%).│
   │ • Active stock-picking risk.      │ • Pure passive index replication. │
   └───────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
```

*Practitioner Workaround:* Investors seeking passive index exposure in Nepal can construct a **Synthetic Personal Index** by buying and holding the top 20–30 blue-chip companies by market capitalization across banking, insurance, hydropower, and manufacturing.

## Lesson 18.5 — Short Selling, Securities Lending, and Margin Trading

### The Missing Triad of Two-Sided Markets

```
                              THE TWO-SIDED TRADING TRIAD
                          ┌────────────────────────┐
                          │   Margin Trading       │
                          │ (Borrow cash to buy)   │
                          └───────────┬────────────┘
                                      │
                 ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
                 ▼                                         ▼
   ┌───────────────────────────┐             ┌───────────────────────────┐
   │    Short Selling          │ ◄────────── │    Securities Lending     │
   │ (Sell borrowed shares)    │             │ (Lend long shares for fee)│
   └───────────────────────────┘             └───────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Margin Trading:** Approved by SEBON under the *Margin Trading Directive* (brokers with NPR 50M+ net worth can finance up to 50% on eligible blue chips). In practice, margin borrowing is still conducted via commercial bank **Share Collateral Loans (शेयर धितो कर्जा)** under NRB single-borrower limits.
2. **Short Selling:** Completely absent on NEPSE. Investors cannot borrow shares to profit from downward corrections.
3. **Securities Lending & Borrowing (SLB):** CDSC does not yet operate an automated custodial lending pool, depriving long-term institutional holders (CIT, PF, life insurers) of risk-free lending fee yields.

## Lesson 18.6 — Strategic Implications: How Missing Instruments Shape NEPSE

### 1. Cash Allocation as Primary Downside Protection
Because put options and inverse funds do not exist, portfolio risk management must be executed through **cash buffering**, position sizing, and disciplined valuation filters.

### 2. Sustained Speculative Mispricings
Without short sellers attacking overvalued companies, speculative momentum can inflate prices far beyond intrinsic value during bull markets, leading to violent multi-month corrections when retail buying exhausts.

### 3. The 1960s Information Edge
Because NEPSE lacks hundreds of sell-side equity analysts and algorithmic options tracking, an individual investor who conducts deep primary research on NFRS quarterly balance sheets, management governance, and field operations retains a **massive structural alpha advantage**.
