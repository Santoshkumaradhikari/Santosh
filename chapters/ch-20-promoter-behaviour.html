Promoter Behaviour — The Most Underanalysed Risk in NEPSE

Related-party transactions, promoter share pledging, and boardroom capture explain more of the permanent capital loss suffered by NEPSE investors than any P/E multiple or technical breakout ever will.

Lesson 20.1 — Two-Tier Ownership Architecture

In Nepal, promoter shares carry a statutory lock-in (3 years for hydropower, 5 years for BFIs under BAFIA) and a mandatory 51% promoter floor in banking. Promoter shares (symbol P) historically trade at a 30%–50% discount to public shares due to illiquidity and transfer approvals.

Lesson 20.2 — Incentive Misalignment

Promoter economics diverge from minority interests through related-party procurement margins, personal share pledging, and at-par rights offerings (issuing shares at Rs. 100 par when trading at Rs. 500+, where promoters absorb devolved shares and dilute retail holders).

Lesson 20.3 — The Promoter Playbook

Key tactics include routing contracts through unbenchmarked private subsidiaries, board packing, and exploiting operating information asymmetry. In 2014, a 51% promoter feud at NCC Bank paralyzed the board for 3 months, forcing central bank intervention.

Lesson 20.4 — Reading Shareholding Trends

Distinguish mechanical dilution from active secondary sell-downs. SEBON requires listed companies to announce the exact end date of a promoter lock-in period 30 days in advance to alert the market of potential supply expansion.

Lesson 20.5 — High-Priority Red Flags

  • Share Pledging (शेयर धितो): Promoters borrowing against shares to fund private ventures creates cascading forced-selling risk upon margin calls.
  • Distressed OTC Transfers: Promoter shares transacted off-market at unusually steep discounts.
  • Unbenchmarked Related-Party Notes: Vague footnotes hiding related-party procurement costs.

Lesson 20.6 — The 8-Point Promoter Scorecard

# Dimension Favourable Signal (Green) Unfavourable Signal (Red)
1 Shareholding Trend Stable or increasing via rights/open market Active OTC sell-down without clear reason
2 Share Pledging Zero or minimal shares pledged for debt Substantial or rising share pledge filings
3 Rights History Fair capital calls, transparent allocation Repeated at-par rights with devolvement capture
4 Board Independence Qualified independent directors Family-dominated board without checks
5 Related-Party Deals Explicit naming & competitive pricing Opaque, aggregated footnote disclosures
6 Dividend Consistency Reliable cash payouts backed by cash flow Shifting payouts tied to promoter cash needs
7 Regulatory Track Record Zero NRB or SEBON enforcement actions History of PCA or supervisory sanctions
8 Research & Disclosure Prompt filings, transparent commentary Minimal statutory disclosures with chronic delays
