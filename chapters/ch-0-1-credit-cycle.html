Nepal’s Credit Cycle: The Hidden Equity Cycle

Price is downstream of liquidity. Liquidity is downstream of credit. Credit is controlled by Nepal Rastra Bank. To understand why NEPSE does what it does, begin here.

The banking and financial complex — commercial banks, development banks, finance companies, microfinance and insurance — constituted roughly two-thirds of NEPSE market capitalisation as recently as 2021 (68.9% of total market cap that year) and has fallen steadily since, to 51.7% for banks, financial institutions and insurance combined as of mid-February 2026, with hydropower rising to 16.0% of market cap over the same period.

0.1.1 — Bank Lending Growth vs. NEPSE Index

Every major NEPSE cycle is driven primarily by NRB's private-sector credit growth. Margin lending amplifies bull markets as higher share prices expand collateral borrowing limits, while credit contraction triggers mechanical forced liquidations.

0.1.2 — Remittance → Deposit → Lending Multiplier

Remittance inflows equal 25%–30% of Nepal's GDP. As remittances enter the banking system, they expand deposit headroom. If the CRR is 4% and the regulatory SLR floor is 10% for commercial banks (8% dev. banks, 7% finance cos.), a bank receiving NPR 100 in fresh deposits can lend up to approximately NPR 86 at minimum compliance, though Nepali banks typically hold more liquidity than the floor requires, so realised lending capacity is usually lower.

0.1.3 — Credit Expansion Lag to EPS Growth

Bank earnings follow credit expansion with a 6 to 12 month lag due to loan disbursement timelines, fixed-deposit repricing schedules, and quarterly reporting delays. Equity markets anticipate reported earnings by 3 to 6 months.

0.1.4 — Credit Contraction & NPL Spikes

Late-stage credit expansion always shifts into NPL surges and aggressive loan-loss provisioning. The index fell from its all-time closing high of 3,198.60 (18 August 2021) to roughly 1,806 by June 2022 — a decline of approximately 45% over 13 months, one of the fastest major corrections in NEPSE's history.

0.1.5 — The 3-Number Monthly NRB Credit Discipline

  • Private Sector Credit Growth (YoY %): Leading cycle indicator (>20% late-stage, 12%–18% healthy, <10% tightening).
  • System-Wide CD Ratio (%): Distance to the 90% statutory ceiling.
  • Weighted Average Interbank Rate (%): Real-time measure of liquidity tightness.
