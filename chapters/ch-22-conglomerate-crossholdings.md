> *"What looked like diversification on the trading terminal was concentration in the ownership registry."*

In NEPSE, an investor holding shares in a commercial bank, an insurer, a hydropower developer, and a manufacturing company may believe they own a balanced portfolio. In reality, all four scrips frequently trace back to the **same promoter family**, the same boardroom, and an interconnected chain of guarantees and share pledges.

## Lesson 22.5 — A Practical 7-Step Due-Diligence Framework

Before committing capital to any NEPSE issuer, execute this **7-Step Diligence Sequence**:

1. **Identify the Ultimate Promoter:** Name the underlying family, trust, or business house behind the controlling block.
2. **Enumerate Group Entities:** List all affiliated listed and unlisted entities across banking, hydro, insurance, and manufacturing.
3. **Audit Related-Party Notes:** Check for recurring intercompany loans, management fees, and cross-guarantees.
4. **Inspect Share-Pledge Filings:** Verify whether promoter shares are pledged as collateral for debt.
5. **Identify the Group's Weakest Link:** Spot the entity with the highest leverage, stalled construction, or largest NPLs.
6. **Stress-Test Contagion Channels:** Determine how a liquidity crunch at the weakest link would spill over to the target company.
7. **Discount Sum-of-the-Parts:** Refuse to sum group market capitalisation without deducting recycled debt.

## Lesson 22.6 — The Diversification Illusion: Sector Labels vs. Balance Sheet Reality

```
                              THE DIVERSIFICATION ILLUSION
   ┌───────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Apparent Sector Diversification   │ Economic Reality: One Family Web  │
   ├───────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
   │ • 1 Commercial Bank               │ • All 4 companies share the same  │
   │ • 1 Life Insurance Scrip          │   promoter family board control.  │
   │ • 1 Hydropower Developer          │ • Intercompany loans link debt.   │
   │ • 1 Manufacturing Company         │ • A single liquidity shock hits   │
   │   (4 Different NEPSE Sectors)     │   all 4 holdings simultaneously.  │
   └───────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
```

> **The Golden Rule:** True diversification in NEPSE requires allocating across **unconnected promoter groups**, not merely across different sector ticker symbols.

### Chapter Summary & Core Takeaways

- [ ] **Cross-Holdings are Structural:** Resulting from the history of private licensing in Nepal.
- [ ] **Beware of Circular Financing:** Scrutinize rights issue subscriptions to ensure capital is fresh equity, not recycled group credit.
- [ ] **Check the Weakest Link:** Distress in a promoter's private project can force margin liquidation on their listed shares.
- [ ] **Diversify Across Promoters:** Ensure your portfolio is not secretly underwritten by a single family empire.
