> *When you purchase an ordinary share, you are not buying a lottery ticket. You are purchasing fractional ownership of a real operating enterprise.*

In Nepal, the primary legal instruments governing share ownership are the **Companies Act, 2063 (2006 AD)**, the **Securities Act, 2063 (2006 AD)**, and sector-specific legislation such as the **Banks and Financial Institutions Act (BAFIA), 2073**. Under the Companies Act, an equity share confers a legally protected right to participate in corporate governance, share in net earnings, and hold a residual claim against assets in liquidation.

## Lesson 13.1 — What You Own When You Buy a Share: Rights, Claims, and Residual Nature

### The Bundle of Four Rights

Ownership of an ordinary share confers four distinct legal and economic rights:

```
                               THE SHAREHOLDER'S BUNDLE OF RIGHTS
   ┌───────────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────────┐
   │    Economic Rights    │   │   Governance Rights   │   │  Information Rights   │
   │ Dividends, bonus      │   │ 1 Share = 1 Vote at   │   │ Right to audited NFRS │
   │ shares, rights issues,│   │ AGM; elect board &    │   │ annual reports and    │
   │ and residual assets.  │   │ ratify auditors.      │   │ quarterly financials. │
   └───────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────┘
```

1. **Economic Rights:** Entitlement to receive declared cash dividends and bonus share allotments, participate in rights issues, and hold the residual claim on assets upon company liquidation.
2. **Governance Rights:** One-share-one-vote power at Annual General Meetings (AGMs) to elect directors, approve annual balance sheets, and vote on major corporate mergers.
3. **Information Rights:** Statutory right to receive quarterly financial disclosures, audited annual reports under Nepal Financial Reporting Standards (NFRS), and material disclosures on NEPSE.
4. **Transferability Rights:** The legal right to sell shares on NEPSE's secondary market through licensed stockbrokers, converting ownership back into cash.

### The Residual Nature of Ordinary Equity

```
                               CORPORATE LIQUIDATION WATERFALL
                 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                 │ 1. Operating Expenses & Employee Wages (Top Priority)      │
                 │ 2. Government Taxes & Statutory Dues                       │
                 │ 3. Secured Debt (Bank Loans, Debentures with collateral)   │
                 │ 4. Unsecured Creditors & General Borrowings                │
                 │ 5. Preference Shareholders (Fixed Capital & Dividends)     │
                 │ 6. Ordinary Equity Shareholders (Residual Claim / Bottom)  │
                 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Ordinary shareholders stand **last** in the hierarchy of corporate claims. In a liquidation or corporate insolvency, ordinary shareholders receive only what remains after satisfying all operating costs, government taxes, secured debt holders, bank loans, and preference capital.

## Lesson 13.2 — Face Value in Nepal: The NPR 100 Standard and Its Accounting Relevance

### The Statutory NPR 100 Standard

In Nepal, the nominal or face value of an ordinary share is almost universally fixed at **NPR 100**. This statutory standard serves as the accounting base for:
- **Paid-Up Capital:** Calculated as $\text{Total Shares Outstanding} \times \text{NPR 100}$.
- **Dividend Percentage Conventions:** When a Nepali company announces a "15% Cash Dividend", it means **15% of face value = NPR 15 per share**, regardless of whether the stock trades at Rs. 200 or Rs. 800 on NEPSE.
- **Bonus Share Allotments:** A 20% bonus share issue transfers NPR 20 per share from retained earnings into paid-up capital at face value.
- **Regulatory Capital Requirements:** Central bank directives (e.g., NRB mandating NPR 8 billion minimum capital for commercial banks) are anchored to paid-up capital at face value.

```
                           FACE VALUE VS BOOK VALUE VS MARKET PRICE
   ┌───────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Metric                │ Meaning & Investor Interpretation                         │
   ├───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Face Value (Rs. 100)  │ Nominal statutory value. Irrelevant for intrinsic worth.   │
   │ Book Value per Share  │ Total Net Worth divided by total outstanding shares.      │
   │ Market Price (LTP)    │ Live clearing price on NEPSE based on supply and demand.  │
   │ Intrinsic Value       │ Discounted present value of expected future cash flows.   │
   └───────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Lesson 13.3 — Market Price vs. Face Value vs. Book Value: The Three Numbers

### Interpreting the Valuation Triangle

```
                                  THE VALUATION TRIANGLE
                                      ▲ Market Price (LTP)
                                     ╱ ╲ (Reflects current sentiment & future ROE)
                                    ╱   ╲
                                   ╱─────╲
                                  ╱       ╲ Book Value per Share
                                 ╱─────────╲ (Historical accumulated retained earnings)
                                ╱           ╲
                               ╱─────────────╲ Face Value (Rs. 100 Statutory Base)
```

1. **Market Price:** The live price determined by continuous order matching on NEPSE. Reflects expected future earnings power, risk premiums, and macro liquidity.
2. **Book Value per Share (BVPS):** 

$$\text{BVPS} = \frac{\text{Paid-Up Capital} + \text{General Reserves} + \text{Retained Earnings} - \text{Accumulated Losses}}{\text{Total Shares Outstanding}}$$

3. **Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio:** 

$$\text{P/B Ratio} = \frac{\text{Market Price per Share}}{\text{Book Value per Share}}$$

When a commercial bank generates a Return on Equity (ROE) exceeding its Cost of Equity, it commands a P/B multiple well above 1.0x (historically 1.2x to 2.5x in Nepal). When non-performing loans rise and ROE drops below the cost of capital, P/B multiples compress toward or below 1.0x.

## Lesson 13.4 — Promoter Shares vs. Public Shares: The 30–50% Price Discount, Lock-In, and Conversion Rules

### The Two Share Classes on NEPSE

```
                           PROMOTER VS PUBLIC SHARE COMPARISON
   ┌───────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Promoter Shares (e.g. NABILP)     │ Ordinary Public Shares            │
   ├───────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
   │ • Founding capital (51–70% equity)│ • Public IPO/FPO float (30–49%)   │
   │ • 3-Year statutory lock-in period │ • Freely tradeable on NEPSE       │
   │ • Trades at a 30–50% price gap    │ • Baseline screen market price    │
   │ • Identical dividends & voting    │ • Identical dividends & voting    │
   └───────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Why Promoter Shares Trade at a 30% to 50% Discount

Promoter shares (traded on NEPSE under designated tickers such as `NABILP`, `CZBILP`, or `GBBLPO`) have **identical economic rights** to ordinary shares: the same Rs. 100 face value, identical cash dividend payouts, identical bonus share allocations, and identical voting power.

Yet, promoter shares routinely trade at a **30% to 50% discount** to ordinary public shares due to:
- **Transfer Restrictions:** Buyer eligibility requirements and NRB source-of-funds verification.
- **Illiquidity:** Lack of active retail daily trading volume.
- **Minimum Block Lots:** Transactions often move in large block auctions rather than single-kitta retail orders.

### Lock-In & Conversion Dynamics

1. **Hydropower & Corporate Issuers:** Under SEBON's single-ISIN framework, promoter shares are locked for **3 years** post-commercial operation/listing, after which they automatically convert to freely tradeable ordinary shares.
2. **Banks & Financial Institutions (BFIs):** Under BAFIA and NRB rules, promoters must hold at least **51%** equity. After 10 years of operation (5 years for insurance companies), promoters can apply for phased conversion of excess promoter shares into ordinary shares with central bank approval.

## Lesson 13.5 — Share Certificates to Demat: The Digital Transformation of Ownership in Nepal

### The Dematerialisation Milestone

In the pre-Demat era, physical share certificates caused extensive settlement friction, signature mismatch disputes, lost certificates, and long transfer delays.

Under the regulatory leadership of SEBON and **CDS and Clearing Limited (CDSC)** (established in 2010), Nepal executed a full transition to electronic book-entry ownership:
- **Beneficial Owner Identification (BOID):** Every investor is assigned a permanent, unique 16-digit electronic account holding legal title to all listed securities.
- **MeroShare & C-ASBA:** Enables investors to view portfolio balances in real time, execute electronic delivery instructions (EDIS) for trade settlements on T+2, and apply for IPOs with funds lien-blocked in their bank accounts via **C-ASBA**.

### Investor Decision Checklist: Analyzing Ordinary Shares

- [ ] **Distinguish Face Value from Value:** Never treat a stock trading near Rs. 100 as intrinsically cheap without examining Book Value and ROE.
- [ ] **Check the Promoter/Public Ratio:** What percentage of total shares is held by promoters (e.g., 51:49 vs 70:30)?
- [ ] **Track Promoter Conversion & Lock-In Expiry:** For hydropower counters, identify when the 3-year promoter lock-in ends to anticipate supply shocks.
- [ ] **Reconcile Demat Ledgers:** Reconcile broker trade confirmations with your official MeroShare BOID statement monthly.
