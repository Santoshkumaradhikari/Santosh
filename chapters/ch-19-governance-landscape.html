Corporate governance in Nepal is not an abstract compliance topic. It is the single most important determinant of whether an investor's capital, once committed, remains their own.

Lesson 19.1 — The Type II Agency Problem in NEPSE

In developed markets, governance addresses the Type I Agency Problem (Dispersed Shareholders vs. Hired Managers). In Nepal, the central danger is the Type II Agency Problem: controlling promoter families owning 51%–70% of equity expropriating minority public shareholders through related-party contracts and perks.

Lesson 19.2 — Legal & Regulatory Architecture

Statutory Requirement Companies Act, 2063 SEBON CG Directive, 2074
Independent Directors Mandatory: 1 (Board ≤7) or 2 (Board >7) Reinforces Companies Act baseline
Audit Committee Mandatory for Capital ≥ NPR 30M; Non-exec chair Mandatory 3-member independent assessment
Chair / CEO Separation Not specified Explicitly barred from being same person
Board Tenure Limit Not specified Maximum 4 years, no extension
Related-Party Family Loans Not addressed Explicitly prohibited
Financial Crime Bar Not specified 10-year bar on directorships

Lesson 19.3 — The NRB Overlay (BAFIA)

Banks and financial institutions face additional oversight under the Banks and Financial Institutions Act (BAFIA), 2073, including CEO/director fit-and-proper educational criteria, a 51% minimum promoter floor, and central bank intervention powers.

Lesson 19.4 — Enforcement Gaps

Written regulations fail when supervisory capacity is constrained, as illustrated by the 2009–2011 real estate BFI crisis, the 2020s cooperative embezzlement scandal (NPR 87B+ in losses), and pervasive pre-announcement insider leakage.

Lesson 19.5 — Forensic Annual Report Due Diligence

  • Cross-check independent director surnames and business ties with the promoter family.
  • Read the Audit Committee report for substantive inquiry vs. generic boilerplate.
  • Verify explicit counterparty naming and pricing terms in related-party disclosures.
  • Track director insider trading patterns under Section 100 disclosures.
  • Verify cash dividend conversion against reported accounting profits.

Lesson 19.6 — Governance Valuation Discount & Position Sizing

Investors must apply a 15% to 35% discount to P/E and P/B multiples on promoter-captured companies and enforce strict portfolio position ceilings (e.g., 0% allocation on active regulatory sanctions, 3% on opaque governance).
