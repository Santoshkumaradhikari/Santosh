Lesson 12.1 — What Market Microstructure Means

Market microstructure examines the mechanics beneath quoted screen prices. In thin frontier markets like NEPSE, three hidden execution costs dominate: the bid-ask spread, market impact (slippage), and adverse selection from informed insider selling.

Lesson 12.2 — Queue Priority Mechanics

Orders in NATS execute on Price-Time Priority. Institutional and broker direct terminals benefit from lower-latency connections compared to retail mobile TMS apps. Operators also use spoofing (placing large fake orders and cancelling them before execution) to manipulate retail sentiment.

Lesson 12.3 — Fake Volume Creation (Wash Trading)

Wash trading involves circular matching between coordinated accounts without changing beneficial ownership. Its primary goal is to manufacture artificial volume breakouts in illiquid scrips, luring retail traders into buying operator inventory.

Lesson 12.4 — Broker-Level Routing Incentives

Using Market Orders gives execution intermediaries discretion over fill prices in thin books. Using Limit Orders protects retail investors by setting a strict price ceiling or floor.

Lesson 12.5 — Price Ramping: The 4-Phase Model

  1. Quiet Accumulation: Operators quietly build positions in dormant scrips over weeks.
  2. Volume Ignition: Wash trades and planted social media rumors trigger the first upper circuit.
  3. Controlled Run: Multi-day upper circuits create artificial scarcity and intense retail FOMO.
  4. Distribution & Exit: Operators dump their entire inventory into pending retail buy queues.

Lesson 12.6 — Information Asymmetry in NEPSE

Information flows from Promoters/Insiders $\rightarrow$ Connected Broker Desks $\rightarrow$ Institutional Funds $\rightarrow$ Public Retail Investors. Buying based on unverified social media tips invariably provides exit liquidity to earlier tiers.

Lesson 12.7 — Circuit Limit Exploitation

Operators exploit the ±10% daily circuit bands by manufacturing upper circuit queues to exit at cycle peaks, or triggering lower circuits to engineer panic selling and accumulate distressed shares at deep discounts.

Lesson 12.8 — Defensive Rules for Retail Investors

The 8 Rules of Microstructure Defense

  • Never use Market Orders in illiquid counters; use Limit Orders strictly.
  • Treat 3+ consecutive upper circuits as a distribution warning, not a buy signal.
  • Demand a verifiable news catalyst for any sudden volume surge.
  • Ignore social media tipping channels and rumor groups.
  • Build illiquid positions in staggered tranches over multiple sessions.
  • Never sell in panic during engineered lower circuit cascades if fundamentals are sound.
  • Treat brokers strictly as transaction execution conduits.
  • Maintain a detailed decision log to track behavioral biases.
