Lesson 14.1 — How a Company Lists on NEPSE

Under the Securities Act, 2063 and SEBON regulations, every public issue must be audited under NFRS, approved by SEBON and sector regulators (NRB for BFIs, Nepal Insurance Authority for insurance), and managed by a licensed Issue Manager (Merchant Banker).

Lesson 14.2 — Prospectus Forensic Analysis

Key audit areas in a prospectus include 3-year NFRS cash flow vs. accounting profit, PPA tariff conditions (hydropower), NPL loan-loss provisioning (banking), revaluation reserve bonus issuances, and auditor emphasis-of-matter notes.

Lesson 14.3 — IPO Pricing: Par Value, Premium, and Book Building

Pricing Method Mechanism Analytical Considerations
Par Value (Rs. 100) Fixed statutory face value base. Does not guarantee undervaluation.
Fixed Premium Issued above Rs. 100 backed by Net Worth. Check whether implied P/E exceeds secondary market peer averages.
Book Building 40% QIB auction determines cut-off price; 60% general public at 10% discount. Institutional price discovery; minimum application is 50 kitta.

Lesson 14.4 — Allotment System: The 10-Kitta Rule & Statutory Quotas

Public issues in Nepal feature statutory reservations: 10% for local residents (hydro/infrastructure), 10% for foreign-employed Nepalis (remittance quota), and 5% for mutual funds. Oversubscribed general public shares are allotted via computerized lottery in minimum lots of 10 kitta with equal probability.

Lesson 14.5 — IPO Application via C-ASBA & MeroShare

Applications are submitted digitally on MeroShare under the C-ASBA system. Funds are lien-blocked in the linked bank account using the investor's CRN (C-ASBA Registration Number) and automatically unblocked if unallotted.

Lesson 14.6 — Listing Day Dynamics

First-day trading is driven by unsuccessful applicant FOMO and allottee price anchoring. Newly listed scrips typically experience initial euphoria followed by a multi-month valuation correction toward fundamental intrinsic value.

Lesson 14.7 — The 6-Layer IPO Analytical Framework

  1. Business Quality: Scalable revenue model and structural growth tailwinds.
  2. Promoter Pedigree: Clean regulatory track record and aligned lock-in terms.
  3. Financial Health: Real cash conversion, solid balance sheet, and low NPLs.
  4. Pricing Fairness: P/E and P/B multiples aligned with secondary peers.
  5. Use of Proceeds: Capital deployed for productive capex vs. passive debt payoff.
  6. Expected Return: Evaluating probability-weighted return on blocked funds.

Lesson 14.8 — Common IPO Traps

Beware of inflated pre-IPO premium valuations, deferred operating expenses, pre-IPO bonus issuances funded by revaluation reserves, and heavily pledged promoter shares.
