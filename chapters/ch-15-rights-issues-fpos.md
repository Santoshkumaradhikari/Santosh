> *"When a company asks existing shareholders for more money, the question is never simply whether to give it — it is whether the terms are fair, the capital will be used wisely, and you are being treated as an owner or a source of funds."*

In Nepal's capital market, the methods through which listed companies raise additional equity follow distinct structural patterns. Two primary instruments dominate secondary capital raising: the **Rights Issue** and the **Further Public Offering (FPO)**. Both mechanisms expand a company's paid-up share capital, and both carry profound mathematical and governance consequences for minority shareholders.

---

## Lesson 15.1 — Why Rights Issues Are the Primary Capital-Raising Tool for NEPSE Companies

### The Structural Drivers of Rights Issues

In developed markets, corporations raise secondary capital through deep corporate bond markets, syndicated institutional placements, or convertible debt. In Nepal, where corporate debt markets are thin and long-term bank loans are expensive, the **Rights Issue** serves as the path of least resistance.

```
                              WHY PROMOTERS PRESERVE RIGHTS ISSUES
   ┌───────────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────────┐
   │ Regulatory Mandates   │   │ Control Preservation  │   │ Lower Underwriting Fee│
   │ NRB & NIA directives  │   │ Promoters maintain    │   │ Cheaper and faster to │
   │ forcing paid-up       │   │ voting control by     │   │ clear through SEBON   │
   │ capital enhancement.  │   │ subscribing in full.  │   │ than public FPOs.     │
   └───────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────┘
```

1. **Regulatory Minimum Capital Escalation:** Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) directives mandating minimum paid-up capital of **Rs. 8 Billion** for commercial banks (and similar hikes by the Nepal Insurance Authority for life/non-life insurers) forced institutions to repeatedly tap shareholders.
2. **Promoter Control Retention:** Unlike public FPOs or strategic private placements, rights offerings allow promoter groups to maintain their exact 51% ownership ratio by subscribing in full.
3. **The Dilution Cost of Non-Action:** If a shareholder does not subscribe to their rights entitlement and does not sell their rights in the secondary market, their ownership stake and per-share equity value are **mechanically diluted**.

## Lesson 15.2 — Rights Ratio, Rights Price, and Theoretical Ex-Rights Price (TERP)

### Core Arithmetic of Rights Pricing

```
                               THE THREE CORE RIGHTS METRICS
   ┌───────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Metric                │ Mathematical Definition & Formula                         │
   ├───────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Rights Ratio (N : R)  │ Entitlement proportion (e.g. 1:2 = 1 new share per 2 old).│
   │ Rights Price (P_r)    │ Offering price (almost universally Rs. 100 par value).    │
   │ TERP (Ex-Rights Base) │ Theoretical post-issue clearing price.                    │
   └───────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The TERP Formula

$$\text{TERP} = \frac{(N \times P_{\text{cum}}) + (R \times P_{\text{rights}})}{N + R}$$

Where:
- $N$ = Number of existing shares required for entitlement
- $R$ = Number of new rights shares issued
- $P_{\text{cum}}$ = Closing market price on the day prior to the Book Closure Date
- $P_{\text{rights}}$ = Rights offering price (typically Rs. 100)

### Worked Example: Himalayan Bank (1:2 Rights Issue)

Assume an investor holds shares trading at **$P_{\text{cum}} = \text{Rs. 400}$**, with a proposed **1:2 Rights Issue** at **$P_{\text{rights}} = \text{Rs. 100}$**:

$$\text{TERP} = \frac{(2 \times 400) + (1 \times 100)}{2 + 1} = \frac{800 + 100}{3} = \frac{900}{3} = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 300}$$

### Value of the Rights Entitlement

$$\text{Value of Right per Existing Share} = P_{\text{cum}} - \text{TERP} = 400 - 300 = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 100}$$

$$\text{Value per New Right Share} = \text{TERP} - P_{\text{rights}} = 300 - 100 = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 200}$$

> **Key Rule:** An investor who does not subscribe to their rights and does not sell their entitlement loses **Rs. 100 per share in uncompensated dilution**.

## Lesson 15.3 — Subscribe, Renounce, or Sell: A Disciplined Decision Matrix

### The 4-Question Analytical Filter

```
                                THE RIGHTS DECISION TREE
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 1. Core Thesis Check ──> Would I buy this stock at TERP if I held no shares?     │
   │ 2. Capital Use Check ──> Productive capex expansion or passive regulatory fill?  │
   │ 3. Valuation Check   ──> Is post-rights P/E and P/B attractive relative to ROE?  │
   │ 4. Portfolio Check   ──> Do I have unencumbered cash without taking on leverage? │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Scenario | Recommended Action | Underlying Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High ROE, Productive Capex (Hydro/Growth)** | **Subscribe in Full** | Incremental capital creates value exceeding the cost of equity. |
| **Stagnant ROE, High TERP Valuation** | **Renounce / Sell Rights** | Avoid deploying fresh cash into low-returning equity. |
| **Cash Constrained Portfolio** | **Partial Subscribe & Sell Balance** | Avoid taking on margin debt to fund rights issues. |
| **Unsubscribed Rights Auction (लिलामी)** | **Sealed-Bid Auction** | Unexercised rights are auctioned to the public via sealed bid. |

## Lesson 15.4 — FPOs in Nepal: Rules, Pricing, and When Companies Use Them

### Further Public Offering (FPO) Mechanics

An FPO is an issuance of new shares to the **general public**, governed by SEBON's *Securities Registration and Issuance Regulation, 2073*:

```
                           RIGHTS ISSUE VS FPO COMPARISON
   ┌───────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Rights Issue                      │ Further Public Offering (FPO)     │
   ├───────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
   │ • Offered to existing holders     │ • Offered to the broad public     │
   │ • Preserves promoter equity ratio │ • Dilutes promoter concentration  │
   │ • Issued at Rs. 100 par value     │ • Issued at premium/market price  │
   │ • Applied via MeroShare C-ASBA    │ • Applied via standard C-ASBA     │
   └───────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Strategic Reasons Companies Issue FPOs
1. **Promoter Share Dilution:** Reducing promoter ownership to meet regulatory public-float minimums (e.g., bringing promoter holding down to 51%).
2. **Institutional Capital Injection:** Bringing new institutional and foreign-employed remittance investors into the shareholder base.
3. **Premium Reserve Capitalization:** Generating substantial share premium reserves to strengthen the balance sheet.

## Lesson 15.5 — The Equity Dilution Problem: Why Frequent Rights Issues Hurt Minority Shareholders

### Return on Equity (ROE) as the Ultimate Diagnostic

$$\text{ROE} = \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Total Shareholders' Equity}}$$

When a company continuously doubles its paid-up capital through rights issues, its net profit must double proportionally just to maintain a steady ROE. 

If paid-up capital grows at **20% CAGR** while net profit only grows at **10% CAGR**, Return on Equity mechanically collapses, dragging down justifiable Price-to-Book (P/B) multiples.

```
                              THE EPS & ROE DILUTION TRAP
   ┌───────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬────────────────┐
   │ Metric                │ Pre-Rights Year      │ Post-Rights Year 1   │ Post-Rights Yr 3│
   ├───────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────┤
   │ Paid-Up Capital (NPR) │ Rs. 5 Billion        │ Rs. 10 Billion (1:1) │ Rs. 10 Billion │
   │ Net Profit (NPR)      │ Rs. 1.0 Billion      │ Rs. 1.2 Billion      │ Rs. 1.3 Billion│
   │ Earnings Per Share    │ Rs. 20.00            │ Rs. 12.00 (Plunge)   │ Rs. 13.00      │
   │ Return on Equity      │ 20.0%                │ 12.0%                │ 13.0%          │
   └───────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴────────────────┘
```

### Pre-Investment Dilution Screening Rule

Before buying any NEPSE stock, compare:
- **5-Year Paid-Up Capital Growth Rate** vs. **5-Year Net Profit Growth Rate**.
- If capital growth outpaces net profit growth, the company is **consuming equity capital**, not creating compounding value for shareholders.

### Chapter Decision Checklist

- [ ] Calculate the **Theoretical Ex-Rights Price (TERP)** as soon as a rights ratio is declared.
- [ ] Determine the intrinsic value of your rights entitlement.
- [ ] Never leave rights unexercised; if you lack cash to subscribe, renounce and sell the entitlement.
- [ ] Screen historical 5-year ROE and EPS growth to identify chronic dilution traps.
