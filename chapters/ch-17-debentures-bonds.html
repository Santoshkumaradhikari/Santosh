Lesson 17.1 — What Is a Debenture: Legal Architecture

Debentures on NEPSE carry a standard face value of NPR 1,000, fixed annual coupon rates (7%–11.5%), and 5-to-10 year maturities. Under the Insolvency Act, 2063, debenture holders sit senior to equity shareholders in the corporate liquidation waterfall.

Lesson 17.2 — Corporate Debentures on NEPSE

Dominated by commercial banks (to meet NRB Basel-III Tier-II capital rules) and hydropower companies (matching predictable PPA tariff cash flows), debentures require independent debenture trustees and SEBON rating approvals.

Lesson 17.3 — Government Securities: PDMO & NRB Auctions

Under the Public Debt Act, 2079, sovereign borrowing is managed by the Public Debt Management Office (PDMO) with the Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) executing Dutch auctions for Treasury Bills (28 to 364 days) and Development Bonds (2 to 15 years).

Lesson 17.4 — Yield, Coupon, and Price: The Inverse Rule

Bond prices move inversely to market yields. For an 8% 5-year debenture (NPR 1,000 face value):

Price @ 10% Yield = NPR 924.07 (Discount)
Price @ 8% Yield  = NPR 1,000.00 (Par)
Price @ 6% Yield  = NPR 1,084.25 (Premium)

Lesson 17.5 — Default Risk & Tax Treatment

Credit quality is assessed by SEBON-licensed rating agencies (ICRA Nepal, CARE Ratings Nepal, Infomerics Credit Rating Nepal). Interest income from listed debentures is subject to a 5% final withholding tax (WHT) for resident individuals under the Income Tax Act, 2058.

Lesson 17.6 — Bond Yields as the Gravitational Anchor of NEPSE

The 364-day Treasury Bill rate serves as the foundational Risk-Free Rate (Rf). When NRB tightens monetary policy and yields spike, equity discount rates rise and NEPSE valuation multiples compress mechanically.
