> *The first trade is the most dangerous one to misunderstand.*

Every listed company was once a private enterprise. Every equity scrip traded on the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) passed through a single foundational gateway: the Initial Public Offering (IPO). This process is not merely administrative paperwork. It is the moment a private company opens its books to public scrutiny and solicits risk capital from citizens.

This chapter provides a complete map of Nepal's primary market ecosystem — from regulatory approvals and prospectus forensic analysis to book-building price discovery, C-ASBA allotment mechanics, and listing-day behavioral traps.

## Lesson 14.1 — How a Company Lists on NEPSE: The IPO Process Step by Step

### The Regulatory Framework

Nepal's primary equity market is governed by the **Securities Act, 2063 (2006 AD)**, the **Companies Act, 2063**, and SEBON's **Securities Registration and Issuance Regulation, 2073**. 

No entity may issue shares to the public without prior approval from the **Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON)** and compliance with sectoral regulatory capital guidelines:
- **Banks & Financial Institutions:** Governed by Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) capital directives under BAFIA.
- **Insurance Companies:** Governed by the **Nepal Insurance Authority (Nepal Bima Pradhikaran)**.
- **Hydropower & Corporates:** Governed by Electricity Regulatory Commission (ERC) guidelines and SEBON listing criteria.

### The Sequential IPO Roadmap

```
                               THE 10-STEP IPO LIFECYCLE
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 1. AGM Resolution  ──> 2. Due Diligence  ──> 3. Draft Prospectus ──> 4. SEBON    │
   │ & Issue Manager Appt   by Merchant Banker     Filing with SEBON     Review Period│
   │                                                                                  │
   │ 5. Permit Issued   ──> 6. C-ASBA Window  ──> 7. Allotment Audit  ──> 8. Listing  │
   │ Public notice out      7–14 days sub.        10-kitta lottery       on NEPSE     │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Step | Milestone Activity | Key Participants | Analytical Significance for Investors |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Board & AGM Resolution** | Company Board, Shareholders | Authorizes public float and selects licensed Issue Manager. |
| **2** | **Merchant Banker Due Diligence** | Issue Manager, Statutory Auditors | Verification of assets, title deeds, PPA contracts, and tax filings. |
| **3** | **Draft Prospectus Submission** | Issue Manager, SEBON | Public filing outlining capitalization, risk factors, and financial records. |
| **4** | **SEBON Review & Scrutiny** | SEBON Capital Market Division | Regulator reviews valuation methodology, disclosures, and credit ratings. |
| **5** | **Issuance of Public Permit** | SEBON, Issue Manager | Final prospectus released; subscription dates announced. |
| **6** | **C-ASBA Subscription Window** | Commercial Banks, MeroShare | Applications submitted; funds lien-blocked in investor accounts via CRN. |
| **7** | **Subscription Close & Scrutiny** | Issue Manager, CDSC | Duplicate applications filtered; oversubscription ratio verified. |
| **8** | **Allotment via 10-Kitta Lottery** | Issue Manager, CDSC | Computerized audited allotment conducted; results published. |
| **9** | **Unblock / Refund Processing** | Commercial Banks, C-ASBA | Lien released automatically on unallotted bank balances. |
| **10** | **NEPSE Listing & Secondary Open** | NEPSE, CDSC | Ticker assigned; open-auction IEP determines first-day opening price. |

## Lesson 14.2 — The Prospectus: What to Read, What to Verify, and What to Question

The prospectus is a legally binding disclosure document under the Securities Act, 2063. Any material misrepresentation exposes company directors and the Issue Manager to statutory liability.

```
                               KEY PROSPECTUS AUDIT SECTIONS
   ┌───────────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────────┐
   │ Objects of the Issue  │   │ 3-Year Audited NFRS   │   │  Related-Party Notes  │
   │ Are funds deployed for│   │ Examine cash flow from│   │ Check for loans or    │
   │ real capex expansion  │   │ operations vs reported│   │ contracts routed to   │
   │ or passive debt payoff│   │ accounting net profit.│   │ promoter entities.    │
   └───────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────┘   └───────────────────────┘
```

### Forensic Checklist for Prospectus Financials

1. **Revenue Quality & Power Purchase Agreements (Hydropower):**
   - Check the PPA tariff rate (e.g., Rs. 4.80 dry season / Rs. 8.40 wet season per unit).
   - Check remaining years on the PPA and transmission line completion risk.
2. **Net Interest Margin & NPLs (Banking & MFIs):**
   - Compare gross Non-Performing Loan (NPL) ratios against NRB's 5% regulatory ceiling.
   - Examine whether loan-loss provisioning has been deferred.
3. **Revaluation Reserves vs. Real Retained Earnings:**
   - Verify whether pre-IPO bonus share capitalizations were funded by genuine operational profits or artificial asset revaluations (land/building write-ups).
4. **Auditor's Report & Key Audit Matters:**
   - Look for **Qualified Opinions** or **Emphasis of Matter** paragraphs highlighting contingent liabilities, disputed tax assessments, or pending litigation.

## Lesson 14.3 — IPO Pricing in Nepal: Par Value, Fixed Premium, and Book Building

```
                                NEPSE IPO PRICING MODELS
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 1. Par Value (Rs. 100)      2. Fixed Premium Model       3. Book Building Method │
   │ Standard statutory base.    Offered at a fixed markup.   40% Institutional QIBs  │
   │ Does not imply cheapness.   Must be backed by net worth. 60% Retail (10% disc.)  │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Par Value Issues (NPR 100)
The standard base price in Nepal. A Rs. 100 issue price does not guarantee an undervalued asset; a company with negative net worth or persistent operating losses is expensive even at Rs. 100.

### 2. Fixed Premium Issues
Companies with proven earnings records may issue shares above par value (e.g., Rs. 250 = Rs. 100 face value + Rs. 150 premium). SEBON requires premium pricing to be supported by historical Net Worth per Share, capitalization multiples, and independent valuations.

### 3. The Book Building Method (बुक बिल्डिङ विधि)
SEBON introduced Book Building to enable market-driven price discovery for profitable real-sector and manufacturing issuers:
- **Institutional Bidding (40%):** Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIBs) submit bids within a designated price band. The clearing price establishes the official **Cut-Off Price**.
- **Public Offering (60%):** General public investors receive shares at a **10% discount to the QIB Cut-Off Price** (with a minimum application size of **50 kitta**).

## Lesson 14.4 — Allotment System: The 10-Kitta Rule, Statutory Quotas, and Lottery Probability

### Statutory IPO Allocation Quotas

SEBON regulations mandate that public offerings be divided into distinct reserved quotas:

```
                            STATUTORY IPO ALLOCATION BREAKDOWN
                 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                 │  Project-Affected Local Residents (Hydro/Infra)   10%      │
                 │  Nepalese Employed Abroad (Remittance Quota)      10%      │
                 │  Collective Investment Schemes (Mutual Funds)      5%      │
                 │  Company Employees                               2 – 5%    │
                 │  General Public Pool (10-Kitta Lottery)          Remainder │
                 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The 10-Kitta Equal Probability Rule

In oversubscribed public issues, SEBON enforces the **10-Kitta Allotment Policy**:
- Every valid applicant is entered into a computerized lottery with **equal probability**, regardless of whether they applied for 10 shares or 1,000 shares.
- Each selected applicant receives a minimum lot of **10 shares (10 kitta)**.
- **Strategic Rule:** In heavily oversubscribed IPOs (e.g., 20x to 80x), applying for more than 10 kitta simply blocks surplus cash without increasing your mathematical odds of being selected.

```
                           LOTTERY EXPECTED VALUE CALCULATION
   ┌──────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Parameter                        │ Hypothetical 10-Kitta Example            │
   ├──────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Issue Price                      │ Rs. 100 per share (Rs. 1,000 blocked)    │
   │ Expected Listing Price           │ Rs. 220 per share (Rs. 120 gain / share) │
   │ Oversubscription Ratio           │ 40x (1 in 40 allotment odds = 2.5%)      │
   │ Potential Gain if Allotted       │ 10 shares × Rs. 120 = Rs. 1,200          │
   │ Expected Mathematical Return     │ Rs. 1,200 × 2.5% = Rs. 30 (3.0% on float)│
   └──────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Lesson 14.5 — IPO Application via C-ASBA, CRN, and MeroShare: The Mechanics

### The Digital C-ASBA Architecture

The **Centralized Application Supported by Blocked Amount (C-ASBA)** system, managed by CDSC, enables secure digital subscription:

```
                                  C-ASBA DIGITAL WORKFLOW
   ┌───────────────────────┐       ┌───────────────────────┐       ┌───────────────────────┐
   │ 1. MeroShare Login    │ ────> │ 2. Enter Bank & CRN   │ ────> │ 3. Automated Allotment│
   │ Select active IPO and │       │ Funds lien-blocked in │       │ Shares to Demat BOID; │
   │ apply for 10+ kitta.  │       │ your bank account.    │       │ lien debited/unlocked.│
   └───────────────────────┘       └───────────────────────┘       └───────────────────────┘
```

### Critical Application Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Insufficient Bank Balance:** The ASBA block fails immediately if your linked bank account lacks sufficient balance at the time of submission.
2. **Expired Demat / MeroShare Renewal:** Accounts require annual renewal with your Depository Participant (DP); expired accounts are rejected during clearing.
3. **Unverified CRN:** Ensure your C-ASBA Registration Number (CRN) is verified and linked to your active KYC bank account.
4. **Duplicate Applications:** Applying across multiple bank accounts under the same citizenship or 16-digit BOID results in automatic disqualification.

## Lesson 14.6 — Listing Day Dynamics: What Typically Happens and the Behavioural Reasons Why

### The Anatomy of First-Day Trading

```
                              LISTING DAY VOLATILITY PATTERN
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Open: Sharp gap-up at upper price band (driven by unsuccessful applicant FOMO).  │
   │ Mid-Session: Profit-taking waves from retail allottees taking listing gains.     │
   │ 3–12 Months: Price gradually deflates toward fundamental intrinsic value.        │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Psychological Forces at Play

1. **Anchoring to Issue Price:** Successful allottees anchor mentally to Rs. 100, rushing to lock in 50–100% immediate paper gains.
2. **Unsuccessful Applicant FOMO:** Non-allottees bid aggressively at the open, mistakenly assuming that because they liked the stock at Rs. 100, it remains a bargain at Rs. 220.
3. **The 3-to-12 Month Mean Reversion:** Globally and on NEPSE, newly listed stocks frequently experience post-listing euphoria followed by a multi-month price drift as quarterly earnings reports test speculative narratives.

## Lesson 14.7 — Analysing an IPO: A 6-Layer Decision Framework

```
                                 THE 6-LAYER IPO FILTER
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Layer 1: Business Model  ──> Clear revenue model and industry growth tailwinds?  │
   │ Layer 2: Promoter Track  ──> Clean regulatory record and aligned lock-in terms? │
   │ Layer 3: Financial Health──> Sustainable margins, clean NFRS notes, low NPLs?   │
   │ Layer 4: Valuation Screen──> Fair P/E and P/B multiples relative to NEPSE peers? │
   │ Layer 5: Use of Proceeds ──> Productive capex vs. passive promoter debt payoff?  │
   │ Layer 6: Expected Return ──> Is expected listing gain worth the capital lockup?  │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> **The Fundamental Synthesis Question:** *Would you buy this stock in the secondary market at the issue price tomorrow if there were no lottery listing premium? If no, you are speculating on market psychology rather than investing in a business.*

## Lesson 14.8 — Common IPO Traps: Overpriced Issues, Window-Dressed Financials, and Promoter Red Flags

### 1. The Overpriced Premium Trap
Issuers in low-margin sectors inflate pre-IPO asset valuations to justify steep premiums. When trading commences, secondary market liquidity quickly discounts the stock back to tangible book value.

### 2. Window-Dressed Financial Statements
- **Deferred Operational Expenses:** Capitalizing routine operating expenses as balance sheet assets to artificially inflate pre-IPO net profit margins.
- **Related-Party Revenue Spikes:** Booking temporary, non-recurring revenue from promoter-owned private affiliates immediately prior to filing.

### 3. Promoter Red Flags
- Promoters with previous records of regulatory censure, loan defaults, or stalled hydro projects.
- Heavy pre-IPO promoter share pledging with commercial banks.
