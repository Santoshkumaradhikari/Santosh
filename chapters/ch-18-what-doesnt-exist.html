The absence of an instrument is not merely a constraint — it is a structural fact that alters every price in the market and every strategy available to every participant.

Lesson 18.1 — Derivatives: The Cash-Only Spot Reality

NEPSE operates exclusively on cash spot settlement (T+2). Without exchange-traded futures or options, investors cannot hedge downside market risk, resulting in sharp, one-directional bear market corrections.

Lesson 18.2 — The ETF Gap

Unlike global markets where low-cost ETFs track benchmarks continuously at NAV, NEPSE lacks Authorized Participant (AP) creation/redemption mechanics. Investors can replicate index performance by constructing a synthetic personal index of top blue-chip companies.

Lesson 18.3 — REITs and Commercial Property

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) remain unlisted due to unstandardized property appraisals, fragmented land titles, and private promoter structures in commercial real estate.

Lesson 18.4 — Commodity Exchanges & Warehouse Receipts

Under the Commodity Exchange Market Act, 2074, SEBON oversees commodity trading infrastructure. Live physical commodity settlement requires accredited storage and electronic warehouse receipt systems (गोदाम रसिद प्रणाली).

Lesson 18.5 — Short Selling, Securities Lending, and Margin Trading

Short selling and Securities Lending and Borrowing (SLB) are non-existent on NEPSE. Margin trading exists under SEBON directives, though leverage is predominantly sourced through commercial bank share collateral loans (शेयर धितो कर्जा).

Lesson 18.6 — Strategic Implications for Investors

Risk management on NEPSE must be achieved through portfolio cash buffering and rigorous valuation filters. Because institutional analyst coverage is thin, diligent primary research provides a substantial edge for fundamental investors.
