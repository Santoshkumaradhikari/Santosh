Markets do not regulate themselves. The history of financial markets worldwide is a history of what happens when they try.

In Nepal, the regulatory architecture governing the capital market is uniquely complex. Multiple institutions exercise overlapping authority over different aspects of market operations, and the boundaries between their respective mandates are tightly intertwined with macroeconomic and sectoral policies. Understanding who regulates what, how those regulators are structured, what powers they wield, and — critically — what political and institutional incentives shape how they exercise those powers, is foundational market intelligence.

Lesson 8.1 — SEBON: Mandate, Powers, Structural Limitations, and Political Economy

What SEBON Is

The Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON) — Dhitopatra Board Nepal (धितोपत्र बोर्ड नेपाल) — is the apex regulatory authority for Nepal's capital market, established on June 7, 1993, and reconstituted under the Securities Act, 2063 (2006 AD). It is an autonomous statutory body under the Ministry of Finance, mandated to regulate and develop the securities market, safeguard investor interests, and uphold market integrity.

SEBON regulates market operators (NEPSE, CDSC), market intermediaries (brokers, merchant bankers, DPs, credit rating agencies), mutual funds, and all listed issuers issuing equity or debt securities to the public.

Lesson 8.2 — NRB: How a Central Bank Becomes the Single Most Powerful Force in NEPSE

Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) is Nepal's central bank, established under the Nepal Rastra Bank Act, 2058 (2002 AD). Although its statutory mandate focuses on macroeconomic price stability and bank supervision, in practice NRB is the single most powerful driver of NEPSE's valuation and liquidity cycles.

NRB Policy Instrument Tightening Effect on NEPSE Easing Effect on NEPSE
Policy Repo Rate Raises borrowing cost; FDs become attractive. Lowers cost of leverage; equities gain yield appeal.
CD Ratio Ceiling (90%) Forces credit curbs; triggers margin deleveraging. Expands credit headroom; spurs loanable funds.
Risk Weight on Share Loans Increases bank capital cost; curbs margin lending. Reduces capital requirement; expands share lending.
Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR 4%) Freezes bank reserves; reduces loanable supply. Releases liquidity into the banking system.
Standing Liquidity Facility (SLF) Tightens interbank cash rates. Injects short-term liquidity into commercial banks.

Lesson 8.3 — CDSC: Clearing, Settlement, and Depository Functions

CDS and Clearing Limited (CDSC), established in 2010 as a wholly-owned subsidiary of NEPSE, manages the central electronic depository for DEMAT accounts, executes multilateral clearing and netting, and handles T+2 settlement in coordination with designated clearing banks (including Global IME Bank as lead clearing bank, NIMB, Prabhu, and Siddhartha) integrated with NCHL electronic payment systems.

Lesson 8.5 — Merchant Bankers and Issue Managers

Merchant bankers act as issue managers and underwriters, conducting financial due diligence, preparing prospectuses, managing C-ASBA subscriptions, and advising corporate boards on public offerings and rights issue pricing.

Lesson 8.7 — Investor Protection Fund and Grievance Redressal in Nepal

The Investor Protection Fund (IPF), managed by SEBON, provides a financial backstop for retail investors in cases of broker insolvency or fraud. SEBON and NEPSE also operate centralized online grievance redressal portals for investor complaints.

Lesson 8.8 — Key Acts Governing the Market: Securities Act 2063, Company Act 2063, BAFIA

Primary Legislation Year Enacted Core Jurisdiction & Key Provisions
Securities Act, 2063 2006 AD Establishes SEBON; regulates public offerings, broker licensing, insider trading prohibitions, and the IPF.
Companies Act, 2063 2006 AD Governs corporate incorporation, AGM voting rights, rights issues, bonus share issuances, and liquidation.
Banks & Financial Institutions Act (BAFIA), 2073 2017 AD Governs licensing of Class 'A', 'B', 'C', and 'D' BFIs; regulates capital adequacy, promoter lock-in, and dividend approvals.
Insurance Act, 2079 2022 AD Replaces Beema Samiti with the Nepal Insurance Authority; mandates capital requirements (Rs 5 Arba for Life, Rs 2.5 Arba for Non-Life).
Securities Registration & Issuance Regulation, 2073 2016 AD Dictates prospectus filing standards, IPO/FPO pricing methodologies, and C-ASBA allotment procedures.

Lesson 8.9 — The Political Economy of Regulation: How Incentives Shape NRB, SEBON, and NEA Decisions

Regulatory behavior in Nepal is shaped by institutional incentives: SEBON's structural bias toward IPO approvals, NRB's delicate balance between inflation management and the political unpopularity of monetary tightening, and Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA) acting as the de facto financial gatekeeper for over 90+ listed hydropower companies through PPAs and transmission infrastructure.

Political Economy Checklist for NEPSE Investors

  • Has there been a recent change in SEBON Chairman or NRB Governor? Anticipate shifts in regulatory stance.
  • Are policy rates, CRR, and margin loan risk weights in an easing or tightening phase?
  • Is banking sector loanable liquidity ample (CD < 80%) or constrained (CD > 88%)?
  • For hydropower holdings, are dedicated evacuation substations and transmission lines operational?
  • Are target companies approaching their 3-year promoter lock-in expiration?
