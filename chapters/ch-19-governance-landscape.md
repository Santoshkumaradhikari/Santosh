> *"Corporate governance in Nepal is not an abstract compliance topic. It is the single most important determinant of whether an investor's capital, once committed, remains their own."*

In a market where promoter families frequently hold 51% to 70% of a company's shares, sit on its board, appoint its chief executive, and control its procurement contracts, the central governance risk is fundamentally different from mature Western exchanges.

## Lesson 19.1 — Why Governance Is the Central Risk in Nepali Equities: Type II Agency Problem

### Type I vs. Type II Agency Risk

> **Key Rule:** In a promoter-controlled company, do not ask *"Is the board independent of management?"* Ask: **"Is the board independent of the controlling promoter family?"**

## Lesson 19.3 — The NRB Overlay: Governance for Banks & Financial Institutions (BAFIA)

For commercial banks (Class A), development banks (Class B), and finance companies (Class C), Nepal Rastra Bank enforces an additional supervisory regime under the **Banks and Financial Institutions Act (BAFIA), 2073**:

1. **Executive & Director Fit-and-Proper Criteria:** Mandates specific master's/bachelor's degrees and a minimum of 10 years of senior executive experience for commercial bank CEOs.
2. **Promoter Floor & Lock-In:** Mandates a minimum 51% promoter shareholding floor, preventing promoters from fully dumping control into the public float.
3. **Prompt Corrective Action (PCA):** NRB retains executive authority to remove boards, suspend dividends, and force leadership resignations during governance violations (e.g., the 2025 supervisory actions at NIC Asia Bank over backdated promoter family deposits and risk-reporting misstatements).

- **Historical Precedents:** The 2009–2011 real estate concentration crisis (where over 70% of BFI loans were tied to speculative real estate) and the 2020s cooperative sector collapse (where over NPR 87 Billion in depositor funds were embezzled by unregulated insider groups) demonstrate that written rules fail without independent, aggressive enforcement.

## Lesson 19.5 — Forensic Governance Checklist for Annual Reports

Before valuing any NEPSE scrip, audit these **8 Forensic Dimensions**:

- [ ] **1. True Board Independence:** Verify whether independent directors share business ties, common addresses, or family connections with the promoter group.
- [ ] **2. Audit Committee Substance:** Check if the Audit Committee report discusses contested accounting treatments, or is merely a one-paragraph boilerplate summary.
- [ ] **3. Related-Party Disclosure Specificity:** Confirm that related-party notes explicitly name counterparties and quantify transaction values for leases, procurement, and consulting fees.
- [ ] **4. Chair / CEO Separation:** Ensure the chairperson and managing director/CEO are separate individuals.
- [ ] **5. Director Shareholding Trends (Section 100):** Track whether directors and promoters are net buyers or sellers prior to major corporate announcements.
- [ ] **6. Regulatory Sanction History:** Screen for public NRB censures, fines, or SEBON inquiries over the preceding 5 years.
- [ ] **7. Auditor Tenure & Qualifications:** Scrutinize unbroken decade-long auditor relationships and review Emphasis of Matter notes.
- [ ] **8. Cash Dividend vs. Reported Net Profit Alignment:** Verify that reported accounting profits translate into real distributable free cash flow and dividends rather than perpetual paper receivables.

## Lesson 19.6 — Governance as a Valuation Discount and Position-Sizing Input

### 1. The Governance Valuation Discount
Reported book value and earnings at a promoter-captured company carry a lower probability of reaching minority shareholders intact. Rational investors apply a **15% to 35% discount to P/E and P/B multiples** on governance-compromised issuers.

### Chapter Summary & Core Takeaways

- [ ] **Type II Agency Problem is Paramount:** Controlling promoters—not hired executives—represent the core risk in NEPSE.
- [ ] **Audit the Audit Committee:** Look for substantive inquiry rather than boilerplate compliance copy.
- [ ] **Discount Multiples for Weak Governance:** Never pay fair-value multiples for promoter-captured entities.
- [ ] **Dynamically Reassess:** Update governance scores at every AGM and quarterly disclosure cycle.
