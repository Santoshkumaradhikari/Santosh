When you purchase an ordinary share, you are not buying a lottery ticket. You are purchasing fractional ownership of a real operating enterprise.

Lesson 13.1 — What You Own When You Buy a Share: Rights, Claims, and Residual Nature

Under the Companies Act, 2063 and Securities Act, 2063, ordinary share ownership embeds four rights: economic rights (dividends, bonus shares, rights issues), governance rights (1 share = 1 vote at AGM), information rights (audited NFRS reports), and transferability rights. Ordinary equity is strictly residual in nature, ranking last behind operating debt, taxes, bank loans, and preference capital in corporate liquidation.

Lesson 13.2 — Face Value in Nepal: The NPR 100 Standard

In Nepal, face value is universally standardized at NPR 100 per share. It acts as an accounting anchor for paid-up capital, dividend percentage conventions (e.g., 15% dividend = Rs. 15 per share), and regulatory capital adequacy rules (e.g., NRB's Rs. 8 Arba capital mandate for commercial banks). Face value does not reflect intrinsic worth.

Lesson 13.3 — Market Price vs. Face Value vs. Book Value

Metric Definition & Formula Analytical Significance
Face Value Fixed statutory nominal value (NPR 100). Legal base for paid-up capital & bonus allocations.
Book Value (BVPS) Total Net Worth divided by total shares. Accounting equity built from retained earnings.
Market Price (LTP) Live continuous clearing price on NEPSE. Reflects current market sentiment, liquidity, and expected future ROE.
Price-to-Book (P/B) Market Price / Book Value per Share. Justified above 1.0x when ROE exceeds Cost of Equity.

Lesson 13.4 — Promoter Shares vs. Public Shares

Promoter shares and ordinary public shares enjoy identical economic rights (same Rs. 100 face value, identical cash dividends, bonus shares, and voting power). However, promoter shares on NEPSE (traded under tickers like NABILP, CZBILP, GBBLPO) trade at a 30% to 50% discount to ordinary public shares due to transfer restrictions and illiquidity.

Conversion Dynamics:

  • Hydropower & Corporates: Promoter shares unlock after the 3-year statutory lock-in period post-listing, becoming freely tradeable ordinary shares.
  • Commercial Banks & BFIs: Promoters must retain a minimum 51% equity stake; after 10 years, they may convert excess promoter shares into ordinary shares subject to Nepal Rastra Bank approval.

Lesson 13.5 — Share Certificates to Demat Transition

Nepal eliminated paper share certificate risks (fraud, loss, transfer latency) through CDS and Clearing Limited (CDSC), established in 2010. Today, all listed equity is held electronically in 16-digit BOID Demat accounts, settled on T+2, and integrated with the MeroShare / C-ASBA IPO application infrastructure.

Investor Decision Checklist

  • Never confuse face value (Rs. 100) with intrinsic value or book value.
  • Examine Return on Equity (ROE) when evaluating bank Price-to-Book (P/B) multiples.
  • Track 3-year promoter lock-in expiration dates for hydropower counters.
  • Reconcile monthly broker trade ledgers with your official MeroShare BOID balance.
