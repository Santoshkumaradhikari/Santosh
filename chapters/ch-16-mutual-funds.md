Nepal's mutual fund industry, operating under the **Mutual Fund Regulations, 2067 (2010)** enacted by SEBON, provides retail investors with institutional diversification, professional management, and regulatory safeguards.

## Lesson 16.1 — Open-Ended vs. Closed-Ended Funds: Structure and Differences

Every mutual fund in Nepal falls into one of two structural categories:

```
                            MUTUAL FUND STRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE
   ┌───────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Open-Ended Schemes                │ Closed-Ended Schemes              │
   ├───────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
   │ • Variable unit corpus            │ • Fixed unit corpus from IPO      │
   │ • Transacted directly with fund   │ • Listed & traded on NEPSE        │
   │ • Priced strictly at forward NAV  │ • Priced by market supply/demand  │
   │ • No fixed maturity date          │ • Fixed maturity (7 to 10 years)  │
   │ • Ideal for monthly SIP investing │ • Opportunity for NAV discounts   │
   └───────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The 4-Tier Governance Architecture in Nepal

SEBON mandates a four-party fiduciary structure to prevent fraud and conflicts of interest:
1. **Fund Sponsor:** Bank or financial institution establishing the trust.
2. **Fund Supervisors (Trustees):** Independent fiduciary panel protecting unitholder rights.
3. **Fund Management Company (FMC):** Licensed merchant banker managing portfolio investments.
4. **Depository:** Commercial bank holding physical and electronic custody of portfolio assets.

## Lesson 16.2 — Available Mutual Fund Schemes on NEPSE & The 5% IPO Advantage

### Scheme Classification by Asset Allocation

```
                                SCHEME ASSET ALLOCATION
   ┌───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Scheme Category           │ Core Portfolio Allocation Mandate         │
   ├───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Equity-Oriented           │ ≥ 65% in NEPSE-listed ordinary shares     │
   │ Balanced / Hybrid         │ 40%–60% Equities + 40%–60% Fixed Income   │
   │ Debt / Income Schemes     │ ≥ 70% in Debentures, T-Bills & Deposits   │
   └───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Structural 5% Statutory IPO Quota

Under SEBON issuance regulations, **5% of every public Initial Public Offering (IPO) in Nepal is reserved exclusively for mutual fund schemes at Rs. 100 par value**. 

This gives mutual fund unitholders immediate, low-cost exposure to high-demand primary listings that retail investors can only obtain via competitive lotteries.

## Lesson 16.3 — NAV vs. Market Price: Discount and Premium Dynamics

### The Mathematics of Closed-End Fund Mispricings

$$\text{Discount (\%)} = \left[ \frac{\text{NAV} - \text{Market Price}}{\text{NAV}} \right] \times 100$$

$$\text{Premium (\%)} = \left[ \frac{\text{Market Price} - \text{NAV}}{\text{NAV}} \right] \times 100$$

### The Discount Convergence Strategy

On NEPSE, closed-ended funds routinely trade at **15% to 25% discounts** to their underlying Net Asset Value. As the scheme approaches its mandatory maturity date (typically 7 to 10 years after listing), market price mechanically converges toward actual NAV upon fund liquidation or conversion into an open-ended fund.

```
                              THE DISCOUNT CONVERGENCE PLAY
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Current NAV per Unit:     Rs. 14.00                                     │
   │ Current Market Price:     Rs. 11.20 (20.0% Discount)                    │
   │ Time to Maturity:         2 Years                                       │
   ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ If portfolio return = 0%: Gain = (14.00 - 11.20) / 11.20 = +25.0%       │
   │ If portfolio grows 10%:   Maturity NAV = Rs. 15.40 -> Gain = +37.5%     │
   │ The 20% discount provides a built-in margin of safety.                  │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Lesson 16.5 — Expense Ratios, Regulatory Fee Caps, and Tax Treatment

### SEBON Statutory Fee Caps

Under the *Mutual Fund Regulations, 2067*, operational fees charged to fund corpus are legally capped:
- **Fund Management Fee:** Capped at maximum **1.50%** of average net assets per annum.
- **Depository Fee:** Capped at maximum **0.20%** of average net assets.
- **Fund Supervisor Fee:** Capped at maximum **0.15%** of average net assets.

### Taxation of Mutual Funds in Nepal

- **Fund-Level Exemption:** Under Section 10 of the *Income Tax Act, 2058*, mutual fund investment income and capital gains are exempt from corporate income tax at the fund level to prevent double taxation.
- **Dividend Distribution to Unitholders:** Cash dividends distributed by mutual funds to resident individual unitholders are subject to a **5% Final Withholding Tax (WHT)**.

## Lesson 16.6 — Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) and Rupee-Cost Averaging

### The Arithmetic of Rupee-Cost Averaging

```
                              3-MONTH RUPEE-COST AVERAGING MODEL
   ┌─────────┬──────────────────────┬─────────────┬──────────────────────────┐
   │ Month   │ Monthly Contribution │ Fund NAV    │ Units Allocated          │
   ├─────────┼──────────────────────┼─────────────┼──────────────────────────┤
   │ Month 1 │ Rs. 5,000            │ Rs. 10.00   │ 500.00 units             │
   │ Month 2 │ Rs. 5,000            │ Rs. 8.00    │ 625.00 units (Buys More) │
   │ Month 3 │ Rs. 5,000            │ Rs. 12.00   │ 416.67 units (Buys Less) │
   ├─────────┼──────────────────────┼─────────────┼──────────────────────────┤
   │ TOTAL   │ Rs. 15,000           │ Avg: 10.00  │ 1,541.67 Total Units     │
   └─────────┴──────────────────────┴─────────────┴──────────────────────────┘
```

$$\text{Average Cost per Unit} = \frac{\text{Rs. 15,000}}{1,541.67 \text{ units}} = \mathbf{\text{Rs. 9.73}}$$

The investor achieves an average acquisition cost (**Rs. 9.73**) below the arithmetic average NAV (**Rs. 10.00**) because more units are automatically accumulated when prices drop.

### Chapter Summary & Practical Takeaways

- [ ] **Open-Ended Funds for SIPs:** Best vehicle for automated monthly wealth building at NAV.
- [ ] **Closed-Ended Funds for Value Investing:** Exploit secondary market discounts exceeding 15% near fund maturity.
- [ ] **Leverage the 5% IPO Quota:** Mutual funds capture institutional allocations across all primary NEPSE issuances.
- [ ] **Watch Hidden Turnover Costs:** High portfolio churning creates indirect execution drags beyond the stated Annual Expense Ratio.
