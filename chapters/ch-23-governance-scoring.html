A governance score is not a substitute for financial analysis — it is a multiplier. A cheap stock with terrible governance is not a bargain; it is a trap waiting for the right catalyst.

Lesson 23.1 — Structured Scoring vs. Gut Feeling

Replacing subjective impressions with a structured scoring framework eliminates recency bias, creates an objective audit trail, and establishes firm position-sizing constraints.

Lesson 23.2 — Nepal-Buildable Scorecard Design

Built from public filings (Annual Reports, AGM notices, SEBON CG reports) without requiring private insider access, emphasizing 3–5 year trends over static snapshots.

Lesson 23.3 — The 6-Dimension Rubric (0–12 Scale)

Dimension Score 0 (Poor) Score 1 (Fair) Score 2 (Strong)
1. Board Independence No independent directors Bare statutory minimum met ≥ 2 independent directors with disclosed criteria
2. Related-Party Deals Opaque/missing notes Aggregated figures only Itemized by counterparty, terms, & board review
3. Promoter Trends Unexplained sell-downs Roughly stable Stable or rising; full rights subscription
4. Disclosure Quality Chronic AGM delays Minor past lapses Consistently on-time AGMs & complete filings
5. Capital Return History Dilutive at-par rights Unremarkable history Consistent cash/bonus policy; fair rights terms
6. Audit & Tenure Qualified opinion; long tenure Clean opinion; long tenure Clean opinion; regular rotation & audit review

Lesson 23.4 — Worked Example: Bank X

Scoring Bank X across all 6 dimensions yields: 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 9 / 12 (Grade B), identifying adequate overall governance with specific monitorable items around board independence and auditor rotation.

Lesson 23.5 — Portfolio Position Sizing & Valuation Haircuts

  • Grade A (10–12 pts): Eligible for full conviction position sizing.
  • Grade B (7–9 pts): Standard allocation with active monitoring.
  • Grade C (4–6 pts): Position capped at 50% normal size; 15%–30% multiple haircut.
  • Grade D (0–3 pts): Auto-disqualification (0% allocation).

Lesson 23.6 — Forensic Boundaries

Public scorecards measure disclosed absence of red flags, not unprovable innocence. They must always be paired with quantitative balance sheet and cash flow forensic analysis.
