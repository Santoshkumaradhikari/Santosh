# Part 0 · Chapter 0.1: Nepal’s Credit Cycle: The Hidden Equity Cycle

**First published 21 Aug 2026 · Last verified 30 Aug 2026**

---

### Part 0: Why This Part Exists Before Part I

Every other investing textbook begins with the market. This one begins with what drives the market — because in Nepal, the relationship between underlying economic forces and equity prices is unusually direct, unusually mechanical, and unusually exploitable once understood.

Most markets have enough depth, participant diversity, and instrument variety that prices reflect many competing signals simultaneously. Fundamental analysis, momentum, flows, sentiment, and macro all interact. No single force dominates.

**NEPSE is not that market.**

NEPSE is a market in which one institution — **Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB)** — and one variable — **the availability of credit** — explain the majority of index movement over any meaningful time horizon. The 2016–17 bull run was a credit expansion cycle. The 2021 boom was a liquidity flood catalysed by COVID-era monetary easing. The 2022 crash was a policy tightening episode. In each case, the direction and magnitude of market movement was more accurately predicted by NRB's credit and monetary instruments than by the earnings reports of individual companies.

This is a structural feature of Nepal's financial architecture:

- **The banking and financial complex** — commercial banks, development banks, finance companies, microfinance and insurance — constituted roughly two-thirds of NEPSE market capitalisation as recently as 2021 (68.9% of total market cap that year) and has fallen steadily since, to 51.7% for banks, financial institutions and insurance combined as of mid-February 2026, with hydropower rising to 16.0% of market cap over the same period. When bank earnings move, the index moves. Bank earnings are almost entirely a function of credit growth, Net Interest Margin (NIM), and Non-Performing Loans (NPL) — all directly controlled or heavily influenced by NRB policy.
- **No Short Sellers or Derivatives:** Price discovery is dominated by retail investors responding to credit availability — when loans are cheap and easy, they invest; when credit tightens, they withdraw.
- **Short Transmission Chain:** Nepal's real economy is heavily import-dependent and remittance-financed. The transmission mechanism is short: NRB instrument $\rightarrow$ bank behaviour $\rightarrow$ economic activity $\rightarrow$ corporate earnings $\rightarrow$ market prices.
- **Margin Lending Amplification:** Margin lending — the direct use of bank credit to buy equities — amplifies both the upswing and the correction in ways that are unique in scale to Nepal's market structure.

> **Core Thesis:** Price is downstream of liquidity. Liquidity is downstream of credit. Credit is controlled by NRB. To understand why NEPSE does what it does, begin here.

## 0.1.1 — Bank Lending Growth vs. NEPSE Index: The Empirical Correlation

The single most important chart any Nepali investor can study is the overlay of NRB's annual private-sector credit growth data against the NEPSE composite index.

```
                              THE CREDIT-TO-EQUITY CYCLE
   ┌───────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┬──────────────┐
   │ Market Phase          │ Credit Growth (YoY)  │ NEPSE Index Behaviour    │ Lead / Lag   │
   ├───────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┼──────────────┤
   │ Early Expansion       │ 15% – 20%            │ Flat to mild uptrend     │ 0–3 months   │
   │ Peak Expansion        │ 20% – 30%+           │ Strong bull run          │ 3–9 months   │
   │ Credit Deceleration   │ 10% – 15%            │ Market stalls, rotation  │ Coincident   │
   │ Credit Tightening     │ Below 10%            │ Correction begins        │ 0–6 months   │
   │ Credit Contraction    │ Negative or near 0%  │ Sharp drawdown           │ Coincident   │
   │ Credit Re-expansion   │ 10% – 15% (recovery) │ Base formation, rebound  │ 3–12 months  │
   └───────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┴──────────────┘
```

### Why the Correlation Holds: Four Structural Pillars

1. **Banking Dominance:** Financial institutions form over half of market capitalization. Credit growth drives Net Interest Margins (NIM) and fee income directly.
2. **Margin Lending Feedback Loop:** Cheap credit $\rightarrow$ investors borrow against shares $\rightarrow$ share prices rise $\rightarrow$ collateral values increase $\rightarrow$ borrowing capacity expands $\rightarrow$ prices rise further. When credit tightens, margin calls force cascading liquidations.
3. **Retail Balance Sheets:** When banks lend freely, surplus household cash flows into NEPSE; when credit tightens, retail liquidity contracts simultaneously across demat accounts.
4. **Corporate Capex Transmission:** Listed non-financials (hydropower, manufacturing, hotels) depend on bank credit for debt-equity project funding and working capital.

## 0.1.2 — Remittance $\rightarrow$ Deposit $\rightarrow$ Lending Multiplier

Nepal's credit cycle is, at its origin, a **remittance cycle**. Remittances equal roughly **25% to 30% of national GDP**, acting as the primary engine of foreign exchange reserves and commercial bank deposit formation.

```
                        THE REMITTANCE-TO-CREDIT TRANSMISSION CHAIN
   ┌───────────────────────┐
   │ Migrant Workers Abroad│
   └───────────┬───────────┘
               │ (Foreign Exchange Inflows: USD / AED / MYR / QAR)
               ▼
   ┌───────────────────────┐
   │ Bank Deposit Growth   │
   └───────────┬───────────┘
               │ (Expands CD Ratio Headroom)
               ▼
   ┌───────────────────────┐
   │ Lending Capacity Rises│ ──> (Credit Multiplier Expands Loan Books)
   └───────────┬───────────┘
               │
               ▼
   ┌───────────────────────┐
   │ NEPSE Valuation Rises │
   └───────────────────────┘
```

### The Multiplier Mechanics

When remittances enter the banking system as deposits, banks hold a portion as **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** and **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)** before lending the remainder.

- If the CRR is 4% and the regulatory SLR floor is 10% for commercial banks (8% dev. banks, 7% finance cos.), a bank receiving NPR 100 in fresh deposits can lend up to approximately NPR 86 at minimum compliance, though Nepali banks typically hold more liquidity than the floor requires, so realised lending capacity is usually lower.

```
                              REMITTANCE & MULTIPLIER VARIABLES
   ┌─────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┐
   │ Variable                    │ Baseline Level                 │ Investment Implication    │
   ├─────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
   │ Annual Remittance Inflows   │ NPR 1.4 – 1.7+ Trillion        │ Primary driver of deposits│
   │ Remittance as % of GDP      │ 25% – 30%                      │ Macro external dependency │
   │ Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)    │ 4.0% (NRB-set)                 │ Direct reserve lock-in    │
   │ Statutory Liquidity (SLR)   │ 10% for commercial banks       │ 8% dev banks, 7% finance  │
   │ CD Ratio Regulatory Ceiling │ 90.0% (NRB mandate)            │ Hard lending ceiling      │
   └─────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┘
```

## 0.1.3 — Credit Expansion Lag to EPS Growth: The 6–12 Month Delay

Bank earnings follow credit expansion with a **6 to 12 month lag** due to three mechanical delays:

```
                                  THE THREE-STAGE LAG
   ┌───────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────┐
   │ 1. Loan Disbursement Lag          │ 2. Quarterly Reporting Delay      │ 3. NIM Lag        │
   │ Takes 1–3 months for credit       │ NEPSE-listed banks report with a  │ Fixed deposits    │
   │ pipelines and mortgages to disburse│ 30–45 day post-quarter lag.       │ repricing delay.  │
   └───────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────┘
```

### The Policy Lag Calendar

| NRB Policy Signal | Loan Book Expansion | Reported EPS Impact | NEPSE Market Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Policy Rate Cut / Easing** | 1–3 Months | 3–6 Months | Front-runs by 3–6 months |
| **CD Ratio Ceiling Relaxed** | 1–2 Months | 3–5 Months | 4–8 Months |
| **Policy Rate Hike / Tightening** | 2–4 Months | 4–8 Months | Sharp immediate to 6 months |
| **Margin Loan Cap Tightened** | Immediate (Margin calls) | 2–4 Quarters | **0–4 Weeks (Violent drop)** |

## 0.1.4 — Credit Contraction and NPL Spike: The Inevitable Phase-Shift

Every credit boom in Nepal ends with **Non-Performing Loan (NPL) spikes and loan-loss provisioning**, compressing earnings:

```
                            THE NPL EARNINGS COMPRESSION SPIRAL
   ┌───────────────────┐     ┌───────────────────┐     ┌───────────────────┐
   │ NRB Tightens      │ ──> │ NPL Ratio Rises   │ ──> │ Loan Provisioning │
   │ Policy / Rates    │     │ Borrowers Default │     │ Wipes Out NIM     │
   └───────────────────┘     └───────────────────┘     └─────────┬─────────┘
                                                                 │
                                                                 ▼
   ┌───────────────────┐     ┌───────────────────┐     ┌───────────────────┐
   │ NEPSE Market Crash│ ◄── │ P/B Multiple      │ ◄── │ Net Profit & EPS  │
   │ Cascades Downward │     │ De-rates Lower    │     │ Collapse          │
   └───────────────────┘     └───────────────────┘     └───────────────────┘
```

### Historical Precedent: The 2021–2022 Correction

- **The Peak:** In August 2021, credit growth surged over 25% YoY, the banking system CD ratio hit regulatory limits, and NEPSE peaked at an all-time closing high of **3,198.60 on 18 August 2021**.
- **The Policy Shift:** NRB introduced strict single-obligor margin loan caps (4/12 crore rule) and tightened the CD ratio ceiling to 90%.
- **The Correction:** The index fell from its all-time closing high of 3,198.60 (18 August 2021) to roughly 1,806 by June 2022 — a decline of approximately 45% over 13 months, one of the fastest major corrections in NEPSE's history.

## 0.1.5 — The 3-Number Monthly NRB Credit Discipline

Investors do not need to read 200-page bulletins. Tracking **three numbers** from the monthly *NRB Banking and Financial Statistics* provides a decisive macroeconomic edge:

```
                           THE THREE-NUMBER MONTHLY DASHBOARD
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 1. Private Sector Credit Growth (YoY %)                                         │
   │    • > 20%: Late-stage boom (prepare to trim)                                   │
   │    • 12%–18%: Healthy expansion (accumulate compounders)                        │
   │    • < 10%: Tightening / bottom formation                                       │
   │                                                                                 │
   │ 2. System-Wide CD Ratio (%)                                                     │
   │    • > 88%: Approaching 90% statutory wall (liquidity stress ahead)             │
   │    • < 82%: High loanable headroom (favorable for equity expansion)             │
   │                                                                                 │
   │ 3. Weighted Average Interbank Rate (%)                                          │
   │    • Spike above policy ceiling: Immediate cash shortage in banking system      │
   │    • Trough near deposit floor: Abundant liquidity seeking equity yield         │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
