A market is not an abstraction. It is a collection of human beings and institutions interacting through the price auction.

NEPSE's participant structure is unusual by global standards. It is one of the most retail-dominated equity markets in Asia, where retail accounts drive 80–85% of turnover, institutional depth is narrow, broker networks have expanded to over 90+ firms, and zero market makers exist.

Lesson 9.1 — Retail Investors: The Dominant Force and the Structural Consequences

Retail investors drive 80–85% of NEPSE's daily turnover. This retail dominance creates distinct behavioral dynamics:

  • Herding & Momentum Cascades: Social media channels (Facebook groups, YouTube, Telegram) accelerate buying or selling momentum.
  • Bonus Share Fixation: High retail enthusiasm for bonus share issuances regardless of underlying economic value creation.
  • Margin Leverage Spirals: Retail over-reliance on share-backed loans amplifies bull runs and deepens bear corrections.

Lesson 9.3 — Mutual Funds in Nepal

Regulated under the Mutual Fund Regulation 2067, Nepal's mutual fund sector manages approximately NPR 35–50 billion across ~35–45 schemes. Closed-end funds routinely trade at a 15–30% discount to NAV on NEPSE due to retail market misperceptions, presenting structured value opportunities for long-term investors.

Lesson 9.4 — Licensed Brokers: Broker Expansion and Commission Rates

SEBON expanded the market beyond the historical 50-broker syndicate by licensing over 40+ new firms across three categories (Limited Brokers, Full Operating Brokers, Stock Dealers), establishing a network of over 90+ licensed brokers.

Lesson 9.5 — Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs)

While SEBON permits up to 30% aggregate foreign ownership in listed companies, Nepal's non-convertible capital account and foreign exchange repatriation documentation requirements have kept actual FII participation below 1%.

Lesson 9.6 — NRN and Migrant Worker Investors

SEBON mandates a 10% reservation of all IPOs for Nepalis employed abroad under formal labor permits, channeling remittance savings into national equity capital. Non-Resident Nepalis (NRNs) can invest via dedicated NRNIA accounts with full statutory capital repatriation rights.

Lesson 9.7 — Promoter vs. Public Shareholders

In most listed companies, promoters hold 51% equity with a mandatory 3-year lock-in period. Key public shareholder risks include supply shocks when 3-year lock-in periods expire, promoter share pledging for commercial loans, and related-party transaction scrutiny.

Lesson 9.8 — Market Makers: The Liquidity Consequences

NEPSE operates with zero designated market makers for equity. Every trade requires an immediate matching counterparty. This structural gap results in wide bid-ask spreads (2–5% in illiquid scrips), multi-day lower circuit exit traps during market crashes, and substantial illiquidity valuation discounts.
