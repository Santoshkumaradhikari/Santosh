From passive wealth-building to navigating discount-to-NAV traps — a complete practitioner's guide to mutual funds on the Nepal Stock Exchange.

Lesson 16.1 — Open-Ended vs. Closed-Ended Funds

Operating under the Mutual Fund Regulations, 2067 (2010), open-ended funds expand and contract directly at NAV without a secondary market listing, whereas closed-ended funds trade on NEPSE with fixed unit counts and 7 to 10 year maturities.

Lesson 16.2 — Available Schemes & The 5% IPO Advantage

SEBON mandates that 5% of every public IPO in Nepal is reserved exclusively for mutual funds at Rs. 100 par value, granting mutual funds instant institutional access to primary market listings.

Lesson 16.3 — NAV vs. Market Price & Discount Convergence

Closed-ended schemes on NEPSE frequently trade at 15% to 25% discounts to NAV. As a fund approaches its final maturity date, market price converges toward Net Asset Value upon wind-up or open-ended conversion, providing an asymmetric margin of safety.

Lesson 16.4 — Fund Manager Evaluation

True managerial skill must be evaluated by separating broad market exposure (Beta) from idiosyncratic excess returns (Alpha), measuring Sharpe ratios and maximum drawdowns across a full market cycle.

Lesson 16.5 — Expense Ratios & Tax Rules

SEBON caps management fees (max 1.50%), depository fees (0.20%), and supervisor fees (0.15%). Mutual funds enjoy tax-exempt corporate status at the fund level under the Income Tax Act, 2058, and cash dividends to resident individual unitholders attract a 5% final withholding tax (WHT).

Lesson 16.6 — Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs)

Automating monthly SIP contributions in open-ended schemes exploits rupee-cost averaging, mechanically accumulating more units when prices decline and lowering the average acquisition cost below the arithmetic average NAV.
