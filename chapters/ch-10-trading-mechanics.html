The way orders are placed, matched, and settled defines the boundary between theoretical analysis and real investment performance.

Lesson 10.1 — The NEPSE Trading Engine: NATS, NOTS, and the TMS Portal

NEPSE operates as a fully automated, order-driven electronic market powered by the central NEPSE Automated Trading System (NATS) and NEPSE Online Trading System (NOTS) matching engine. Orders are entered via broker Trade Management Systems (TMS) and matched strictly on Price-Time Priority without designated market makers.

Lesson 10.2 — Trading Hours, Pre-Open Session, and Market Timing

Session Time Window (NST) Functional Rules
Pre-Open Session 10:30 AM – 10:45 AM Order entry within ±5% band; continuous calculation of Indicative Equilibrium Price (IEP).
Pre-Open Auction 10:45 AM – 11:00 AM Orders match simultaneously at single clearing IEP.
Continuous Trading 11:00 AM – 3:00 PM Real-time continuous order matching on price-time priority.
Market Close 3:00 PM Market closes; trade files generated for CDSC settlement.

Lesson 10.3 — Order Types Available: Market Orders and Limit Orders

NEPSE supports Limit Orders (price-guaranteed execution) and Market Orders (immediate execution with high slippage risk). Advanced order types like Stop-Loss, Good-Till-Cancelled (GTC), and Bracket Orders do not natively exist in the exchange engine and must be managed manually as Day Orders.

Lesson 10.4 — The Order Book: Bids, Asks, Spreads, and Depth

NEPSE provides a live Top-5 Market Depth view. The minimum price tick is Rs. 0.10 (10 Paisa), and intraday orders during continuous trading are restricted to within ±2% of the Last Traded Price (LTP).

Lesson 10.5 — Price Circuit Limits: Daily Bands and Market-Wide Halts

Individual listed ordinary shares are subject to a ±10% daily price band relative to the previous day's close. Market-wide index circuit breakers trigger standardized cooling-off halts:

  • 4% index movement in 1st hour: 20-minute market halt.
  • 5% index movement in 2nd hour: 40-minute market halt.
  • 6% index movement at any time: Trading closed for the remainder of the day.

Lesson 10.6 — T+2 Settlement Cycle and the 20% Close-Out Penalty

Trades settle on a standard T+2 business day cycle. If a seller fails to transfer sold shares via EDIS on MeroShare before the settlement deadline, CDSC levies a mandatory 20% Close-Out Penalty on the total trade value, credited directly to the buyer as compensation.

Lesson 10.7 — MEROSHARE: Demat, EDIS, and C-ASBA

MeroShare is CDSC's digital backbone for managing 16-digit BOID demat accounts, calculating Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC), approving EDIS transfers, and applying for IPOs/Rights via the C-ASBA system using a Bank-linked CRN (C-ASBA Registration Number).

Lesson 10.8 — Broker-Assisted vs. Self-Directed TMS Trading

While broker terminals assist high-net-worth clients, self-directed web/mobile TMS trading eliminates communication latency, provides direct order book depth visibility, and ensures instant execution.

Lesson 10.9 — Absence of Short Selling

NEPSE does not permit short selling. Because investors cannot borrow shares to profit from overvaluation, negative views cannot be directly expressed through trading, creating a systematic upward bias and allowing speculative bubbles to persist longer than in developed markets.

Lesson 10.10 — Dividends, Bonus Shares, and Rights Issues

Corporate Action Taxation / Pricing Adjustment Critical Investor Rule
Cash Dividend 5% TDS withheld at source for individual investors. Must hold shares on or before the Book Closure Date.
Bonus Shares Price adjusts automatically on Ex-Date based on bonus ratio. Additional units credited directly to Demat BOID post-AGM.
Rights Issues Priced typically at Rs. 100 face value; dilutes non-subscribers. Apply via MeroShare C-ASBA during the subscription period.
