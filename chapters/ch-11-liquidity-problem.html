A market is only as useful as its ability to let you exit. Without liquidity, a market price is merely an opinion.

Lesson 11.1 — Why Liquidity Risk Is the Foundation of All NEPSE Investment Decisions

In NEPSE, liquidity is the primary variable that determines whether an investment thesis can be executed in practice. Liquidity encompasses three dimensions: speed, price impact, and order book depth. Investors face three distinct risks: market-wide turnover collapse, stock-specific illiquidity, and position-size exit traps.

Lesson 11.2 — Average Daily Turnover on NEPSE

NEPSE reached an all-time record single-day turnover of NPR 29.95 billion (Rs 2,995 crore) on August 15, 2024. In typical baseline markets, turnover ranges from NPR 4 to 8 billion, while during monetary tightening and bear phases, daily volume contracts to under NPR 1.5–2 billion.

Lesson 11.3 — Floating Stock and Lock-In

Headline market capitalization is deceptive because only 30% to 45% of shares represent freely tradeable public float. Promoter and strategic institutional shares are subject to statutory 3-year lock-in periods. When lock-in periods expire, sudden secondary market supply surges can cause sharp price corrections.

Lesson 11.4 — The Impact of Thin Liquidity on Price Discovery

Without market makers, thin liquidity produces artificial volatility where small retail orders trigger circuit swings, spreads widen to 2–5%, and stocks enter multi-day lower circuit traps during market selloffs.

Lesson 11.5 — Sector-Wise Liquidity

Sector Category Liquidity Characteristics Typical Order Book Depth
Commercial Banks Deepest institutional float; tight spreads (0.2–0.5%). Deep two-sided books; block trades absorbed.
Hydropower Highest retail turnover & velocity (often 30–45% of daily volume in rallies); vulnerable to bear-phase freezes. High velocity in bull runs; illiquid in selloffs.
Insurance Moderate liquidity; supported by statutory capital consolidation and Ashadh fiscal year-end rebalancing. Medium depth; wider spreads in small non-life.
Manufacturing & Trading Lowest liquidity; few listed companies (e.g., Bottlers Nepal, Salt Trading); very thin public float. Severely shallow; trades infrequent.

Lesson 11.6 — Practical Consequences: Why Large Positions Cannot Be Exited Quickly

Prudent position sizing requires that daily liquidation should not exceed 10% of a stock's 30-day Average Daily Volume (ADV). Positions requiring more than 10–15 trading sessions to liquidate carry significant exit risk and must be sized conservatively.

Lesson 11.7 — Liquidity Cycles in NEPSE

NEPSE turnover is driven by:

  • NRB Monetary Policy: Policy repo rate cuts, interbank liquidity surplus, and margin lending rules.
  • Earnings Seasonality: Corporate dividend declarations and audited financial publications peak between Ashwin and Poush/Magh (October to January).
  • C-ASBA IPO Blocking: Temporary lien holds on retail bank deposits during mega IPO issuances.

The NEPSE Investor's Liquidity Checklist

  • Check the 30-day Average Daily Volume (ADV) before committing capital.
  • Ensure total position size does not exceed 10–15 days of normal volume capacity.
  • Track upcoming 3-year promoter lock-in expiration dates for hydropower holdings.
  • Use Limit Orders to avoid severe execution slippage in shallow order books.
  • Maintain cash buffers during monetary tightening cycles.
